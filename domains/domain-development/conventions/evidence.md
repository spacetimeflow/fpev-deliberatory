# Evidence: what makes a line trustworthy

Every normative line in a domain's conventions ends with one mark. [run]

| Mark | Means | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| `[owner]` | The owner said or ruled it | Binding unless the owner changes it |
| `[run]` | Observed in recorded work | Good for that kind of work |
| `[external]` | Comes from an outside authority or source | As good as the source; cite it and date the check |
| `[inferred]` | Reasoned, not yet observed | Weak; must say what would falsify it |

## Rules

- An `[inferred]` line states `Falsified if …`. [external]
- A number that came from one machine or one project says so. [run]
- A vendor's or an agent's claim is not `[run]` until checked here. [run]
- A quotation is checked against its source by script. [run]
- Weak lines are the first to revisit and the first to cut. [external]

## Kinds of check, and what each can show

- **Form checks** show that the parts are present. [run]
- **Conformance checks** show that a run followed the procedure. [run]
- **Self-verification** checks procedure and never findings. [run]
- **Fresh-reader tests** show that the files can be followed without their author. [run]
- **Planted faults** show that a check can fail. [run]
- **Outside references** show that a check measures the right thing. [run]
- **The owner's use** is the only test of whether the domain serves its purpose. [owner]

Never report a check in stronger words than what it measured. An audio check that compares duration cannot call speech "clean". [run]

## Felt regressions

When the owner says something is worse and every check passes, believe the owner first and look for what the checks cannot see. [owner] List what changed between the two setups before blaming any one factor. [run]
