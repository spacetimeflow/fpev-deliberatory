# Verifying a student's version

Evidence marks: `[owner]` the advisor's words or ruling · `[run]` observed in recorded work · `[external]` an outside authority · `[inferred]` reasoned, with what would falsify it.

## What it was for

- Read the student's prompts and framing first, and state what problem the version set out to solve. [owner]
- If the deliverables were not defined, say so. That is the first point of feedback. [owner]
- Outline what the version actually did, phase by phase, and mark what goes beyond the prompts. [owner]

## Provenance

- Check file dates and history. A whole version produced in one sitting is likely an agent's elaboration. [owner]
- Compare with earlier versions; do not take the latest as best by default. [owner]

## Data and method

- Ask why these data were chosen (the site, day, set) and on what reference. [owner]
- Check the data against their public description and known limits. [run]

## Conclusions

- List the version's conclusions and trace each to its evidence. [owner]
- Where theory meets data, compare them side by side, figure by figure, and note what is missing. [owner]
- Start with conceptual comparison. Statistical testing comes later, when asked. [owner]
- Do not generate new figures or results inside the assessment; if a comparison is not available, say so. [owner]

## Contradictions

- Collect every contradiction between assumptions, results and data. Revealing them is a deliverable. [owner]
