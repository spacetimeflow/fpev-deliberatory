# Student research advising

A domain shape for advising a student whose research is also done with AI agents. Read this first. The `conventions/` say how.

## Purpose

To verify each version a student produces, steer the next one, and keep the research the student's. In the owner's words, an advisor must first ask whether "deliverables [were] carefully defined", notice when a report "seems to contain much more than what's prompted", "reveal all contradictions", and ask whether "this can be simplified".

## Scope

Reviewing a student's version, setting up the next, and turning both into feedback. It includes conference or journal reviews of the student's paper when advisor and student work through them. Writing the student's paper for them is out of scope.

## The situation

A student with an agent can produce a long report overnight. It may answer a question nobody asked, rest on data whose choice nobody explained, and conclude more than its evidence supports. Because it is fluent and full of figures, it reads as progress. The advisor's time is short, and the student learns only from feedback they can act on.

The advisor's verification is itself research, and it often grows the theory. That growth belongs in the advisor's own versions, credited and shared, not mixed silently into the student's.

## Deliverable

For each student version: an assessment of what it set out to do, what it did, and whether its conclusions follow. It comes with the contradictions found, a side-by-side comparison where theory meets data, and a short list of points for the student. When the advisor develops the work further, a new version in the advisor's space is shared back.

## Stages and roles

FPEV's stages apply, alternating: a student version is one cycle, and the advisor's verification of it is another.

- **The student** owns their versions, their data and their next steps.
- **The advisor** frames each verification, decides what matters, and decides what to tell the student.
- **Agents** read the student's prompts and outputs, check provenance, compare, find contradictions, and draft feedback.


| Stage | Criterion | The domain supplies | Stays with the person |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame | understandability | what the version was for; its prompts; the advisor's questions | the questions that matter |
| Plan | conformance | what to compare; which checks; the verification version | approving the plan |
| Execute | quality | provenance, data choices, side-by-side comparison, contradictions | nothing routine |
| Verify | insight | the contradiction list; the side-by-side; draft points for the student | what to tell the student; the next direction |
## Cadence

One verification per student version, usually before a meeting. The advisor's attention goes to the questions and the meeting. Meeting notes and handwritten ideas feed the next framing.

## Record

The student's versions as received, the advisor's verification versions, meeting notes, and the feedback sent.

## Kernel

These hold in every cycle:

- **Know what the version was for** before judging what it found.
- **The student's prompts are the reference.** Flag work beyond them.
- **Conclusions trace to evidence,** and theory meets data side by side.
- **Do not create results inside an assessment.** New work is a new version in the advisor's space.
- **Reveal contradictions; prefer the simpler account.**
- **Check provenance.** Do not take the latest version as best by default.
- **Steer, do not take over.** Feedback goes to the student in a form they can act on.
- **The student's unpublished work stays between advisor and student.**

## Envelopes

Agents may act alone inside these bounds. Outside them, the advisor decides.

- Read and copy the student's versions into the advisor's verification space.
- Draft assessments, comparisons, contradiction lists and short feedback.
- Search published literature on the data and methods.

Agents may never write to the student's repository, contact the student, or share the student's work.

## What to internalize, and what stays on file

**Internalize:** what this version was meant to answer; the difference between the student's result and their agent's elaboration; and that one clear contradiction teaches more than many figures.

**Keep on file:** comparison checklists, data-choice questions, and the record of past versions.

## Untested

- Whether the alternating cadence fits inside a domain. If departures recur in a second advising relationship, it should graduate to its own shape.
- Whether it serves students who do not use agents.
