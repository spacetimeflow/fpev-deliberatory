# Evaluation letters

A domain shape for evaluating a person on an institution's behalf, and writing the letter or report that carries the evaluation. Read this first. The `conventions/` say how.

## Purpose

To write evaluations efficiently while keeping them the evaluator's own, faithful to the facts, and fitted to what the institution asked. In the owner's words, a letter starts from a sample "with minimal but necessary updates", and an edit should "revise as little as possible, only if it's necessary".

## Scope

Recommendation letters, external promotion or tenure reviews, internal merit and appraisal committees, and nominations or award selections. Reviews of manuscripts are a separate domain.

## The situation

An evaluation affects a person's career, and it is signed by someone whose judgment is being trusted. The institution sets the question: its standard, its period, its form. The candidate's materials are confidential, and so are other evaluators' views. Several people are often involved, each on their own schedule.

An agent can draft a persuasive letter in minutes. The risks are a letter judged against the wrong standard, a wrong fact about the candidate, the agent's opinion standing in for the evaluator's, and acting before others have spoken.

## Deliverable

The letter or report in the form the institution requires, ready for the evaluator to revise and sign, with the facts checked. For committee work it also includes the consolidated drafts and a single list of inconsistencies and open questions, each addressed to the person who can answer it.

## Stages and roles

The four FPEV stages apply to each case.

- **The evaluator** states the charge and their judgments, decides the wording that matters, signs and sends.
- **Agents** read the charge and guidelines, check facts against the candidate's materials, draft from the template, find inconsistencies, and prepare emails.
- **Colleagues and staff** contribute views and rulings in their own time.


| Stage | Criterion | The domain supplies | Stays with the person |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame | understandability | the charge and standard; the template; the required form; who else contributes | the reading of the standard; date and letterhead |
| Plan | conformance | what waits for whom, and when | approving the plan |
| Execute | quality | facts checked against the candidate's materials; drafts from the template; inconsistency lists | nothing routine |
| Verify | insight | the draft with changes from the template marked; the facts list; open questions | the judgment, the wording, signing and sending |
## Cadence

Frame settles the charge, standard, template and form in one exchange. The work then waits for others whenever their input is due. Verify is the evaluator reading, revising and signing.

## Record

Each case keeps the charge and guidelines, the evaluator's judgments as given, the checked facts, the drafts, the version actually sent, and the correspondence that settled questions.

## Kernel

These hold in every case:

- **The charge sets the standard.** Evaluate against what the institution asked, not the evaluator's own institution or record.
- **The evaluator's judgment is the evaluation.** Agents record it and check it; they do not supply their own.
- **Every fact about the candidate is checked against the candidate's own materials.**
- **Start from the evaluator's template and change only what must change.**
- **Use the form the institution requires.**
- **Wait for other people's input.** Never act ahead of a colleague, a committee or the person who must answer.
- **Confidential stays confidential.** Never write in a shared folder unless asked, and never move candidate materials out of the case.
- **The evaluator signs and sends.**

## Envelopes

Agents may act alone inside these bounds. Outside them, the evaluator decides.

- Read the case materials in the case's private folder.
- Draft letters, consolidations, inconsistency lists and emails, marked as drafts.
- Search public sources to check public facts, such as a publication's venue.

Agents may never contact candidates, colleagues or staff, write to a shared folder without being asked, or send anything.

## What to internalize, and what stays on file

**Internalize:** what the institution asked; whose judgment each sentence carries; and that a wrong fact in a letter harms a person.

**Keep on file:** templates, guideline checklists, the form requirements, and past corrections.

## Untested

- Whether one domain serves both single letters and committee work. They share evidence and confidentiality rules but differ in pace.
- Whether the facts check catches errors that matter. It found real ones in two cases out of seven.
