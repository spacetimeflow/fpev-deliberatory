# Research proposals

A domain shape for a research grant's life with agents: proposing, responding to reviews, reporting progress, and keeping the project's record. Read this first. The `conventions/` say how.

## Purpose

To turn research into fundable proposals and honest reports without overstating it. In the owner's words, unproven properties "should all be presented as proposed work", and a progress report is "not so technical", within its limit, and free of "internal version numbers/dates".

## Scope

The investigator's own proposals and grants: drafting and revising proposals, recording reviews received, progress and final reports, and project records after an award. Reviewing others' proposals borrows the article-review domain.

## The situation

A proposal is a promise judged by strangers under a sponsor's rules. A paper is not a proposal: the sponsor prescribes its structure, limits and required sections, and reviewers look for one clear thesis and an honest line between what is known and what will be done. Collaborators own their parts. After an award, reports are short forms, read quickly, by people who do not use the team's internal labels.

An agent can produce polished pages fast. The risks are overstated results, a sponsor's rule missed, a collaborator's part written over, and a report padded with speculative work.

## Deliverable

A proposal in the sponsor's format and within its limits, with each claim's status marked (established, preliminary, proposed). It comes with a storyline check and a map from the investigator's comments to the changes made. A report answers the form's questions within its limits. Afterward come the submitted texts, recorded verbatim, and updated project records.

## Stages and roles

The four FPEV stages apply to each proposal or report.

- **The investigator** sets the thesis, decides what is claimed, comments, and submits.
- **Co-investigators** own their parts.
- **Agents** read the solicitation, restructure, check claims and limits, draft, and keep records.


| Stage | Criterion | The domain supplies | Stays with the person |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame | understandability | the sponsor's rules; the thesis; who writes what | the thesis; what is claimed |
| Plan | conformance | the outline in the sponsor's structure; deadlines; limits | approving the plan |
| Execute | quality | restructuring, claim-status marking, compiling, limit checks | nothing routine |
| Verify | insight | the comment-to-action map; the claim-status table; the storyline check | comments; submitting |
## Cadence

The deadline sets the pace. Frame fixes the solicitation, the thesis and who writes what. Verify is one or two rounds of the investigator's comments, mapped to changes. Reports are short single cycles.

## Record

The solicitation and its checklist, each version, the comment-to-action maps, the submitted texts, reviews received verbatim, and the project's records after an award.

## Kernel

These hold in every cycle:

- **The sponsor's rules first:** structure, limits, required sections.
- **Mark every claim's status.** What is not proven is proposed work.
- **One thesis threads through the whole proposal.**
- **Collaborators write their own parts.**
- **Reports are short, plain and honest.** Report only what is ready, and use no internal labels.
- **Check numbers and identifiers.**
- **Record what was submitted, and update the records.**
- **The investigator submits.**

## Envelopes

Agents may act alone inside these bounds. Outside them, the investigator decides.

- Read the solicitation and the investigator's own papers and records.
- Draft, restructure, and check limits, compilation and citations.
- Prepare text for pasting into a sponsor's form.

Agents may never submit, write to a sponsor, or change a collaborator's text without them.

## What to internalize, and what stays on file

**Internalize:** the line between established and proposed; the thesis in one sentence; and that a report is read in a minute.

**Keep on file:** the sponsor's checklists, page and word limits, templates, and the project's record.
